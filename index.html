Reference-free evaluation of NL→FOL translation

Peer Agreement as a Reference-Free Signal of NL→FOL Faithfulness, and Where It Fails

TL;DR. When no gold formula exists, can you tell whether an LLM's first-order logic translation says what the sentence says? We let nine LLMs formalize the same sentence and score each formula by how many of the others an SMT solver proves logically equivalent to it, without ever reading predicate names. On 450 fresh sentences this peer agreement score reaches 0.827 AUROC against a blinded LLM panel and 0.874 against solver labels, where a gemini-2.5-flash judge reaches 0.791 and 0.727. It is a complement, not a replacement: its gain on top of the judge is about 0.03 AUROC with an interval that touches zero, and on sentences where most systems share an error it falls below chance, to 0.419.

Contributions

01

A name-blind agreement score, frozen before evaluation

It ignores predicate names and weights systems by reliability. On 450 fresh sentences it reaches 0.827 AUROC on panel labels and 0.874 on audited solver labels, against 0.791 and 0.727 for a gemini-2.5-flash judge.

02

An estimate of what it adds

Stacked on judge, roundtrip and structural scores it adds +0.033 AUROC on panel labels (95% CI [−0.001, 0.066]) and +0.118 on solver labels. It does not beat binary Dawid–Skene agreement (0.830), and its direction and error-typing parts add nothing.

03

A mapped failure boundary

Exactly invariant to predicate renaming, where the judge loses 0.151 AUROC on random-token vocabulary. It falls to 0.419 on sentences whose majority meaning is wrong, and to 0.705 against the judge's 0.927 on long legal definitions.

04

A clear use: flagging wrong benchmark gold

With the shipped gold as a tenth peer, the score flags wrong gold at 0.837 to 0.859 AUROC and adds +0.070 over a judge run on the gold alone.

05

Negative results for three other families

A monotonicity signature, judgment worlds built by a solver, and instance consequence NLI with a small model: none beats the judge on current systems.

Method

Correct FOL is not unique. Two systems can name predicates differently, split a concept differently, or write logically equivalent formulas, and both be right. Comparing with a gold formula fails twice over: benchmark gold is itself often wrong, and a faithful candidate need not be equivalent to it. The one source of evidence that needs neither the gold nor the text is the other systems. If several independent systems formalize the same sentence, a solver can compare their outputs with each other.

  1. What goes in

    A candidate formula F from one system and peer formulas P1 … Pm: the outputs of the other systems for the same sentence. No gold, no shared vocabulary. The score may read the peers but never the text, the gold or predicate names.

  2. Align without names

    For each peer, search for a map from its predicates and constants into those of F, using structure only. First an arity-preserving bijection; failing that, an injective partial map that leaves some predicates unmatched, provided at least 50% of the peer's predicate occurrences are mapped. The partial map admits peers that decompose a concept differently.

  3. Let the solver decide the relation

    Pairs are first compared on 64 fixed random finite structures of domain size 2 and 3, which refutes equivalence soundly and cheaply. Undecided pairs go to an unbounded Z3 query over an uninterpreted sort, then to grounding over domains up to size 3. The outcome is EQUIV, STRONGER, WEAKER, INCOMPARABLE, CONTRADICTORY or UNALIGNABLE. Because maps never consult names, any consistent renaming leaves the result unchanged.

  4. Weight systems by reliability, without labels

    EQUIV links a sentence's parseable outputs into equivalence clusters. A one-coin Dawid–Skene model fitted over these clusters across all sentences returns one reliability weight per system, floored at 0.05.

  5. What comes out

    The weighted share of covered peers that are equivalent to the candidate, with UNALIGNABLE peers kept in the denominator:

    PA(F) = Σj wj · 1[rel(F, Pj) = EQUIV] / Σj wj

    An unparseable candidate, or one with fewer than two covered peers, gets 0.5 and stays in every evaluation. The same machinery scores a benchmark's gold formula as a tenth system, which flags wrong gold with no extra model call.

Why it is built this way

The configuration was frozen on 700 development sentences before any fresh label existed, chosen from a 12-configuration grid by the mean of panel and solver AUROC, with ties within 0.005 going to the simpler configuration. It uses bijections and partial maps, keeps UNALIGNABLE peers in the denominator, and uses Dawid–Skene weights. Two further components were tested and left out: granularity definitions, which let one predicate stand for a conjunction of up to two literals, and a strength profile meant to name the error type.

Data and labels

  • Systems. Nine LLMs from seven families (llama-3.1-8b, qwen-2.5-7b, mistral-small-3.2-24b, gpt-4.1-mini, gemini-2.5-flash, deepseek-v3.1, gemma-3-27b, phi-4, gpt-oss-120b), one zero-shot prompt with free predicate naming, temperature 0.
  • Development set. 700 sentences (250 MALLS, 250 FOLIO v2 training stories disjoint from both validation versions, 200 ProverQA medium and hard), with 45.1% in the top complexity tercile. Code for this dataset. Used only for configuration and mechanism tests.
  • Fresh set. 450 new sentences (MALLS 170, FOLIO 150, ProverQA 130), half in the top tercile, zero string or story overlap with development. 4,050 greedy candidates, of which 222 are unparseable and kept.
  • Legal set. 158 sentences from the EU AI Act, GDPR, DSA, DMA, Data Act and the SARA tax-code clauses, binned by exception and condition markers into 0–1 (91), 2–3 (51) and 4 or more (16). Code for the legal experiment. Greedy parse rate there is 73.3%.
  • Two label families, both required. Panel labels: a blinded panel of claude-haiku-4.5, grok-4.3 and glm-4.6 (Cohen κ = 0.57 against human corrections on 99 MALLS items). Audited solver labels: solver equivalence to gold after the panel audits it. Solver labels share the score's equivalence checker; panel labels can favour LLM judges. Two checks rule out the simplest artefacts. The score does not merely reproduce the solver label: inside the 168 panel items the solver calls not equivalent to audited gold, it still separates faithful from unfaithful at 0.776 [0.679, 0.858], against 0.738 for the judge. Nor does it merely reward conventional names: it beats the vocabulary conformity control by +0.152 on panel labels and +0.083 on solver labels. Across nine systems, per-system mean scores track per-system faithful rates at Kendall τ = 0.889 under solver labels and 0.556 under panel labels (judge: 0.722 and 0.833); with nine systems these are descriptive.

The added signal is small and ties an existing agreement score

Added to the base of judge, parse rate, roundtrip NLI, roundtrip cosine and structural metrics, peer agreement raises panel AUROC from 0.886 to 0.919. The +0.033 gain has a 95% interval that just includes zero; a directional test at the 5% level gives a lower bound of +0.005. The paper reads this as an increment of about 0.02 to 0.03 AUROC on short sentences, at the edge of significance under panel labels.

Granularity definitions also have a mechanical cost: a definition of the form Q := ¬P absorbs a single negation (detection 0.511 with definitions, 1.000 without), a definition that adds a conjunct absorbs a dropped restrictor (0.659 against 0.995), and the definitions add 11.9% spurious equivalences between formulas from unrelated sentences. Agreement is exactly invariant to vocabulary

Because alignment never reads names, renaming cannot move the score. On 223 development sentences with faithful gold, each gold was rendered in the modal peer's vocabulary, in random tokens and in WordNet synonyms, against 739 operator mutants per rendering. Peer agreement without granularity definitions separates faithful formulas from mutants at 0.999 AUROC in every rendering. The LLM judge falls from 0.945 with conventional names to 0.794 with random tokens and 0.763 when the faithful formula uses random tokens and its mutants use conventional names. The vocabulary conformity control collapses to 0.176 in that crossed condition.

On real candidates, over 1,695 solver-verified meaning-preserving rewrites of 400 fresh candidates, the false alarm rate was 0.25% for synonym renamings, 0.50% for random renamings, and zero for contrapositive, De Morgan and reorder rewrites. The vocabulary conformity control changed on 86.4% of synonym renamings. On 104 sentences paired with entity-renamed paraphrases, the judge's mean score dropped by 0.096 while its AUROC did not change significantly (−0.022 [−0.067, 0.030]).

Agreement fails when most systems share an error

Where a sentence's modal cluster is faithful, peer agreement reaches 0.883 [0.822, 0.934] against the judge's 0.782. Where it is not (61 items), peer agreement falls to 0.419 [0.292, 0.557], below chance, while the judge holds 0.786. Majority clustering does the same (0.867 and 0.433). Mode correctness is read from the labels, so this split is descriptive.

A controlled dose test isolates the mechanism. For 200 development sentences, k of the eight peers were replaced by renamed copies of a mutant. Detection falls from 0.988 at k = 0 to 0.791 at k = 2, 0.409 at k = 4 and 0 at k = 8 when peers are replaced in random order, and reaches 0 already at k = 4 when faithful peers go first. Renamed copies were recognized as equivalent in 502 of 502 audited pairs, so the boundary is arithmetic of peer weight, not a property of the solver. On real development data, concordance within sentences is 0.831 when wrong-meaning clusters hold under a quarter of the peer weight and 0.449 when they hold three quarters or more (logistic slope −2.08 [−2.55, −1.61]); the modal cluster is wrong in 284 of 700 sentences.

Complexity and long legal definitions

On the fresh set, under panel labels, the score's lead over the judge shrinks with complexity: +0.088, +0.107 and −0.028 in the bottom, middle and top terciles (slope −0.058 [−0.135, 0.022], not significant). It is weakest with two or more quantifiers (0.659 against the judge's 0.778) and strongest with three or more conditions (0.871 against 0.787). Public corpora are rich in connectives, not exceptions: FOLIO contains one unless or except in 2,656 sentence-formula pairs.

On the legal set the result is clearly negative for agreement. Peer agreement reaches 0.705, the LLM judge 0.927 and the frontier judge 0.852, and the score trails the judge in every marker bin. Stacked on judge and parse rate it adds +0.021 [−0.001, 0.046]; over the full base, +0.008 [−0.012, 0.028]. Equivalence is rare on long text (4.8% of legal pairs against 38.5% on fresh sentences), and in 76% of the 62 sentences with a labelled modal cluster, that cluster is unfaithful. There the score fell to 0.624 while the judge held 0.946.

Agreement flags wrong benchmark gold

Treating the shipped gold as a tenth peer gives a reference-free flag for wrong gold. On ProverQA, 19 of the 25 golds it scores lowest are wrong, at a 17.5% base rate. On the 450 fresh golds its precision is 0.80 among the 50 it scores lowest.

Other reference-free families do not beat a judge on current systems

On 2023-era Logic-LM outputs for the FOLIO validation split, labelled by solver equivalence to gold, the monotonicity signature and the judgment worlds both ranked candidates above an LLM judge (signature code, worlds code). On current systems the order reversed. The signature's polarity comparison added nothing beyond its own lexical alignment (+0.011 [−0.010, 0.031]) and named real error types less often than the majority class (11.1% against 24.9%). The worlds lost to the judge in the top complexity tercile by −0.098 [−0.184, −0.014], the regime where they had led. The judge that collapsed on the older screen's top tercile (0.552) held 0.772 there on current outputs.

Cost

When peer outputs already exist the score needs no API call and about 0.16 s of solver time per item. Generating eight peers costs about $0.00039 per short item, 13 times the judge's $0.00003, and $0.0019 per legal item, 31 times the judge. With a system's own samples as peers it reduces to sampling self-consistency (0.842 against 0.843 on fresh panel items).

Limitations

  • Thin increment. The increment under panel labels over the base of judge and roundtrip scores is +0.033 with a 95% confidence interval of [−0.001, 0.066]. It is significant only under a directional test, and it matches, rather than exceeds, what latent-class agreement showed on development data. The fresh panel has 256 items (Kish effective sample size 202).
  • No gain over an existing estimator. Peer agreement does not beat binary Dawid–Skene agreement on panel labels, and its gain over majority clustering comes from reliability weighting, not from anything new in the comparison.
  • LLM labels. Panel labels are LLM judgments, with κ = 0.57 against human corrections on MALLS and 0.37 pairwise agreement on the primary error type. No human adjudication was used. On legal text the labels come from the sentence alone, κ falls to 0.17 on sentences with four or more markers, and the panel's sensitivity to exception errors is uncertified; the legal conclusions are correspondingly weak.
  • Substituted and missing baselines. Roundtrip baselines on the fresh and legal sets used a local Qwen3-8B back-translation, which favoured outputs of Qwen models (0.968 against 0.766 roundtrip NLI AUROC on the 18 panel items generated by Qwen models). The frontier judge was not run on fresh items. GenV, FormalAlign and the relabelling framework of Brunello et al. were not benchmarked.
  • Operating conditions. The score needs several independent systems per sentence and cannot score a lone output beyond what self-consistency gives. It fails under shared error, which is frequent on long text.
  • Signal between sentences. On development solver labels, shuffling labels within sentences leaves the score at 0.70 AUROC against a true 0.82, so much of its item-level AUROC separates easy from hard sentences rather than candidates within a sentence. The fresh within-sentence comparison (0.929 against 0.786 for the judge over 70 pairs) used pairs sampled from one modal and one minority output per sentence, a design that favours agreement.
  • Error types. No reference-free method in the study named real error types better than always predicting the most frequent type.
